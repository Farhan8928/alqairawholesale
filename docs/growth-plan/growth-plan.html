The plan on one page

Win the Eid 2027 season with a small, sharp range — then build from repeat orders.

Wholesale is not retail with bigger orders. You sell to perhaps 30–100 shop owners instead of thousands of shoppers, so a few relationships decide the year. The business wins on three things: a short range that sells through, prices that leave the shop a real margin, and being in stock before the season rather than during it.

Five decisions only you can make — needed in the first two weeks

  • Make or buy. Own production through job-work tailoring units, or buy finished garments and put the ALQAIRA label on them.
  • Launch cities. Your home city plus two others you can reach by train in a day.
  • Credit policy. Recommended: advance payment for every first order; credit only after three paid orders.
  • Final prices. The website currently shows working prices that must be confirmed against real costs.
  • Business structure. Proprietorship, LLP or private limited — decide with your CA before applying for GST.

What this means in practice

  • Launch stock must exist by mid-November. A shop that is shown samples in November and told "delivery in January" will buy elsewhere.
  • Plan the last reorder cut-off for around 10 January, so goods reach shops before Ramadan begins.
  • Sizes 56 and 58 sell out first every Eid, a specialist thobe retailer reports. Check this against your own first orders, and make more of the fast sizes.
  • Eid al-Adha is the second chance. Whatever you learn in the first season, apply it to April's orders.

Other dates worth diarising

  • CMAI National Garment Fair, Mumbai, held each July (2026 edition: 13–15 July). Thousands of trade buyers; book a stall for 2027 once you have one full season behind you.
  • CMAI Kidswear fair, Mumbai, held in June.
  • Umrah season runs year-round, with peaks in Ramadan and the school holidays — steady demand for plain white thobes.
  • GST returns monthly or quarterly depending on the scheme your CA chooses.

02

Range, supply and photography

Oct – early Nov

Goal: a short range of garments you can reproduce at the same quality, at a known cost, and photographs of the real thing.

Launch with 8–12 styles, not 50

A new wholesaler's biggest risk is dead stock. A short range lets you make each style deeply enough in every size, keep reorders in stock, and learn quickly what sells. A suggested starting mix — adjust it with your suppliers' knowledge of what sells in your cities:

StyleWhy it is in the launchShare of stock
Plain white thobes — Saudi, Emirati, OmaniYear-round demand: Friday, Umrah, everyday. Lowest risk; sells in every city.40%
Kids thobes in the same cutsFathers buy for sons at the same visit. Cheap to make; lifts order value.20%
Kurta pajama & pathani in 2–3 solid coloursBroad Indian market, Friday and Eid wear.20%
One embroidered / designer jubbaThe Eid and wedding piece. Higher price, higher margin, but make fewer.10%
Abaya or Nehru waistcoatTest one women's or layering piece before committing to a full line.10%

Make or buy?

Own production (fabric bought by you, stitched by job-work units) gives the best margin and control, but needs cash for fabric in advance and someone checking quality in the unit.

Buying finished goods from established manufacturers — Bhiwandi and Mumbai have a concentration of Islamic-wear makers — is faster and needs less supervision, but margins are thinner and the same design may reach your competitors.

A practical start: buy or co-develop the plain thobes from one reliable maker, and produce the one or two signature designs yourself.

Before approving a supplier

  • Get a sealed sample of each style. Every bulk batch is checked against it.
  • Write down the fabric, GSM, lining and opacity. Buyers ask "is it see-through?" before anything else.
  • Measure the size chart on the actual sample: length, chest, sleeve.
  • Agree lead time and minimum per colour, in writing.
  • Wash-test one piece for shrinkage and colour bleed.
  • Keep a second supplier for the plain white thobes, your volume line.
Photography is not optional. The website currently uses AI-generated images from the earlier build. A shop owner who receives garments that do not match the photograph returns the whole consignment and does not reorder. Photograph the approved samples — front, back, detail, fabric close-up — on a plain background, and replace every image before the first sale.
03

Prices and terms

Before the first sale

Goal: prices that give the shop a reason to stock you and give ALQAIRA a margin that survives discounts, freight and slow payers.

Price from the shelf backwards

Start with the price a shopper will pay (the MRP printed on the tag), then work back. Indian garment retailers typically expect to earn around half of the MRP. Worked example using the plain Saudi thobe — the figures are the website's working prices, shown here to illustrate the method, not final numbers:

Per piece₹Note
MRP printed on the tag1,499What the shopper pays, including GST
ALQAIRA wholesale price690Plus 5% GST charged to the shop
Shop's gross margin≈ 780About 52% of MRP — enough to justify shelf space
Your target landed cost≤ 450Fabric, stitching, trims, label, polybag, carton share
ALQAIRA gross margin240≈ 35% of the wholesale price

Build the discounts in from day one

The website offers 5%, 10% and 15% off for orders of 50, 150 and 400+ pieces. At 10% off, the thobe above sells for ₹621 and your margin falls from ₹240 to ₹171. If your real cost is higher than ₹450, reduce the discounts before launch — not after a buyer has seen them.

Watch the GST line

Since 22 September 2025, garments sold at ₹2,500 or less per piece attract 5% GST; above that, 18%. Wholesale prices sit well below this, but an embroidered jubba with a high MRP should be checked — the rate follows the value of each sale.

Recommended trade terms for the first year

First order100% advance, or 50% advance and the balance before dispatch.
Minimum order₹15,000 value, any styles mixed; one size-set pack minimum per style.
CreditOnly after three paid orders; 30 days; a written limit per buyer.
FreightPaid by the buyer, at actuals, by the transporter they choose.
ReturnsManufacturing defects only, reported with photos within 7 days.
SamplesSingle pieces at wholesale price; value adjusted on the first bulk order.
Why credit is the danger. Retail buyers commonly take 30 to 60 days to pay, and often longer than agreed. Every rupee owed to you by a shop is a rupee you cannot spend on fabric for the next batch. Most small wholesalers that fail are profitable on paper and out of cash in practice.

What to say in the first sixty seconds

"We are ALQAIRA, a thobe and kurta wholesaler. One pack is one piece in every size, so you never get stuck with odd sizes. This thobe sells at ₹1,499; you buy it at ₹690 — about ₹780 margin a piece. Where to focus visits

Start with markets where Islamic menswear shops cluster and footfall peaks before Eid. Well-known examples include Mohammed Ali Road and Bhendi Bazaar in Mumbai, the Charminar area in Hyderabad, Aminabad in Lucknow, Jama Masjid and Chandni Chowk in Delhi, Zakaria Street in Kolkata, Shivajinagar in Bengaluru, and Kozhikode in Kerala. Choose the three you can serve best from your base.

From the first order

Goal: every order handled the same way, every rupee collected, and stock you can count at any moment.

Weekly routine

  • Monday: stock count by style and size; list what must be reordered from suppliers.
  • Tuesday–Thursday: visits, follow-ups and new-buyer calls.
  • Friday: one WhatsApp broadcast — new stock, restocks or last delivery dates.
  • Saturday: collections review. Call every buyer who is past due, the same day.

Records to keep from the first day

  • A buyer register: shop, city, GSTIN, contact, orders, payment behaviour.
  • Stock by size, not just by style — the size curve decides reorders.
  • Sell-through by style after each season: what to repeat, what to drop.
  • Every supplier batch checked against the sealed sample, with notes.
06

Growing after the first season

Mar 2027 onward

Goal: a business that grows from reorders and referrals rather than from new visits alone.

1. Review the season honestly

Within two weeks of Eid al-Fitr, list every style with units sold, units left and reorders. Drop the bottom two styles, deepen the top three, and fix the size curve for Eid al-Adha orders in April.

2. Reward the buyers who reorder

Offer credit terms, early access to new designs and priority dispatch to the buyers who paid on time. They are cheaper to keep than new ones are to find.

3. Add cities through distributors

Once a city has five or more active shops, one local distributor who holds stock can serve them faster than dispatches from your godown.

4. Private label

Make your styles under a larger retailer's or reseller's own label, from around 100 pieces per design. It fills production capacity between seasons.

5. Export to the GCC

The UAE, Saudi Arabia and the rest of the GCC are the largest thobe markets in the world, and the Indian community there buys Indian-made kurtas and pathanis.

  • IEC from DGFT to export.
  • RCMC from the Apparel Export Promotion Council (AEPC) for garment exporters.
  • India–UAE CEPA (in force since May 2022) gives many Indian goods preferential or zero duty in the UAE. Claim it with a Certificate of Origin through the DGFT portal.
  • Saudi Arabia applies the GCC unified tariff — most goods around 5% — and clears imports through the SABER platform. Check the exact HS code rate before quoting.

Start with one importer or trader in Dubai or Sharjah rather than shipping speculatively.

6. Add B2B marketplaces

Udaan and Flipkart Wholesale reach small retailers across India. Add them once your stock and dispatch are reliable — marketplace penalties for late dispatch are strict.

The cash cycle

Count the days between paying your supplier and being paid by the shop. That gap is how much money the business needs, and it returns every season.

Advance terms in the first year can halve the money the business needs. Seasonal working-capital loans (drawn before the season, repaid after) suit this business better than long fixed loans; Udyam registration helps with MSME lending.

Seven mistakes that end new wholesale businesses

  • Giving credit to first-time buyers.
  • Launching with too many styles, too thinly stocked.
  • Making every size in equal numbers.
  • Missing the pre-season buying window.
  • Selling from photographs that are not the real garment.
  • Terms agreed verbally instead of on the proforma.
  • Discounts that were never checked against real cost.
